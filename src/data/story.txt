Chapter 1 — The Black Banner

In the kingdom of Velmor, winter arrived with iron skies and starving wolves. Prince Kael Varrow stood beside his father during the Festival of Crowns while thousands filled the capital streets with lanterns.

Then the gates opened.

The king’s most trusted general, Lord Seradin, rode into the city carrying a black banner—the ancient signal of treason.

By midnight, the palace burned.

Kael watched his father die beneath Seradin’s blade while the royal guard turned against the throne. His younger sister vanished in the chaos. Before dawn, Kael escaped through underground tunnels with only a wounded stablemaster and a sword too heavy for his hands.

Behind him, Velmor belonged to traitors.

Chapter 2 — The Forest of Graves

Kael fled north into the cursed wilds known as the Forest of Graves. Trees twisted like skeletal fingers, and spirits whispered beside frozen rivers.

For six days he wandered hungry and half-mad.

On the seventh night, he encountered a blind witch named Mireya living inside the hollow remains of a giant tree. She spoke his name before he offered it.

“You survived because vengeance still breathes in you,” she said.

Mireya revealed a secret buried for centuries: Seradin had not acted alone. Kael’s own uncle had financed the betrayal in exchange for the throne.

Hatred replaced grief.

And hatred gave Kael purpose.

Chapter 3 — The Wolf Mercenaries

To reclaim a kingdom, Kael needed soldiers.

In the lawless border city of Duskport, he sought the infamous mercenary company called the Iron Wolves. Their captain, Rhea Thorn, laughed at the starving prince who offered promises instead of gold.

Then Kael defeated her champion in single combat.

The tavern fell silent as blood spread across the wooden floorboards.

Rhea saw something dangerous in him after that—not nobility, but fury sharpened into discipline.

She agreed to follow him.

For a price.

Chapter 4 — The Broken Crown

Kael learned that his uncle, Edric Varrow, now wore the crown while Seradin ruled the armies.

But the kingdom was rotting.

Villages vanished under impossible shadows. Travelers spoke of ancient creatures awakening beneath the mountains. Taxes doubled. Public executions filled the capital squares.

The people feared their rulers more than famine.

Kael realized betrayal had unleashed something worse than civil war.

It had awakened old magic.

Chapter 5 — The Dragon Beneath Stone

Deep within the ruins of Karoth Deep, Kael searched for a legendary weapon once wielded by Velmor’s first king.

Instead, he found a dragon.

Not a beast of fire and rage, but an ancient creature chained beneath black stone. Its scales shimmered silver in torchlight.

“I know your bloodline,” the dragon said.

Its name was Vaerith, and it revealed the truth: Seradin served a forgotten god sleeping beneath the kingdom. The coup was never about power.

It was a sacrifice.

Thousands would die to awaken what lay below Velmor.

Kael freed the dragon.

And the mountain trembled.

Chapter 6 — The Siege of Hollow Keep

Kael’s army struck their first major victory at Hollow Keep. Snowstorms swallowed the battlefield while Vaerith descended from the clouds like living lightning.

Walls shattered.

Men burned.

For the first time, the usurpers understood the prince still lived.

But victory carried a cost. During the battle, Kael discovered a spy among his closest allies. One of the Iron Wolves had secretly informed Seradin of every movement.

Kael executed the traitor himself.

Afterward, he could no longer sleep.

Chapter 7 — The Sister in Red

Inside a captured fortress, Kael uncovered a terrible truth.

His sister, Lyra Varrow, was alive.

Seradin had raised her within the enemy court, shaping her into a prophet through forbidden magic. She now served the throne willingly, dressed in crimson robes and speaking visions that drove armies to fanaticism.

When Kael finally faced her, she smiled without warmth.

“You should have died with father,” she said.

The words wounded him more deeply than any blade.

Chapter 8 — Night of Betrayal

As Kael’s forces marched toward the capital, Lord Seradin offered peace.

Rhea warned it was a trap.

Kael ignored her.

That night, assassins infiltrated the camp. Fires spread through the tents while soldiers slaughtered each other in confusion. Seradin’s army descended from the hills under moonlight.

Thousands died before dawn.

Worst of all, Kael discovered Rhea had vanished.

Some claimed she abandoned them.

Others whispered she had sold them to the enemy.

Kael believed both.

Chapter 9 — The God Below

The final battle erupted beneath the capital city itself.

Ancient tunnels led into a massive underground cathedral where Seradin completed the ritual to awaken the forgotten god Morvath.

The creature rose like a mountain of shadow and bone.

Reality fractured around it.

Seradin welcomed the apocalypse with tears in his eyes, believing destruction would cleanse humanity of weakness.

Kael fought through collapsing stone and rivers of fire until he finally reached the general who destroyed his family.

Their duel lasted only moments.

Hatred had trained Kael too well.

He drove his sword through Seradin’s heart.

But Morvath continued rising.

Chapter 10 — Ashes and Silence

To stop the god, Vaerith revealed there was only one choice: dragonfire fused with royal blood could seal the darkness again.

The cost would be death.

Kael accepted immediately.

Before the ritual began, Lyra appeared beside him one final time. Her loyalty to Seradin had shattered after witnessing the horror beneath the city.

For the first time in years, she wept.

Brother and sister completed the sealing together.

The capital survived.

The kingdom survived.

But when dawn rose over Velmor, both heirs of the royal bloodline were gone.

Years later, travelers still spoke of two figures walking the distant northern snowfields beside a silver dragon.

Some stories end with revenge.

Others begin there.
