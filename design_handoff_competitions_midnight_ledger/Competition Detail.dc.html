The brief
Somewhere in your story, a stranger does something they will never be thanked for.
That is the whole of it. The kindness can be small enough to miss — a held door, a covered bill, a lie told well. What we're looking for is the shape of the life around it: who needed it, what it cost, and what the world looked like an hour later.
Any setting, any period, any register. We have no appetite for sentimentality and a great deal of appetite for restraint. If your last line explains the story to us, cut it.
“Write the story a stranger needs at three in the morning. Then take out everything that isn't it.”
Maren Whitlock, judge
